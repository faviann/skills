Size a `/to-tickets` slice by the risk it carries, not only by the two capacity budgets.

`<vertical-slice-rules>` bounded a slice only by capacity — one fresh context window and one reviewable pull request. Slices that cleared both still introduced several independent production models at once, and two tickets could quietly claim the same mechanism, so the overlap surfaced as integration work only after both had been published. Three positive rules now apply: risk decides **whether** a slice splits, a safe and useful seam decides **where** it can land, and each semantic case and production mechanism gets exactly one owning ticket. A conditionally-loaded `CALIBRATION.md` carries the risk taxonomy and worked examples. A risk verdict is never cancelled: where no seam exists, a prefactor ticket creates one, and where none can be created the decomposition goes back to the user — who may direct publication anyway, in which case the ticket ships as `ready-for-human` rather than being reclassified as agent-ready. Wide refactors stay the one exception, sequenced as expand–contract: name the batches that consume the compatibility form, and retire the old form in the contract ticket. The docs page names all three rules and the wide-refactor exception.
